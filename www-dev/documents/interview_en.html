What practices are most effective to drive improvements in quality? Computers are tools that extend human information processing functions. Therefore, in principle, programs should be written by users who "know what they want to do," in other words, users with a specific purpose. This is crucial because it allows for a very fast feedback loop in development, progressing from defining the development purpose, to designing, coding, testing, evaluation, revising the development purpose, back to designing, and so on. When "communication with stakeholders" is inserted into this loop, the cost of cycling through the loop increases exponentially as the number of stakeholders grows. This is the reason why large projects fail. In actual development, it's often difficult for users to write the software themselves. Hence, it's preferable to develop with a team of about 1-3 people who understand the purpose well, especially in the initial stages of development. By doing this, quality is automatically ensured. Beyond the initial stages, the cost of cycling the loop inevitably increases. Still, this can be mitigated through an "integration of authoritarianism and liberalism." Specifically: - Multiple groups of developers with the same functions proceed with the development as they see fit in a development environment (liberalism). - A senior programmer, overseeing all these groups, will compare the outcomes from each group. If they find something good, they can unilaterally decide to promote it to the production environment (authoritarianism). - This hierarchical structure of liberalism and authoritarianism can be nested.
